Gender architecture done right

The Lisa Cassidy axis: distributed female power (not the lone special girl), earned competence, romance between peers, stakes without plot armor, misogyny treated as subject not seasoning, moral ambiguity extended to women. The opposite of the ACOTAR / Fourth Wing mode you rate in the 2s and 3s. Badly documented in reviews, so these are reasoned from content — confidence flagged per pick.

Calibration check (you've read these): Tigana, the Farseer trilogy, Novik's Uprooted & Spinning Silver, and The Goblin Emperor all landed as read-and-loved — which is the proof this axis tracks something real in your taste, not genre-matching. So the picks below are all confirmed new to you, pointed at the same architecture. A related thread worth naming from your Goblin Emperor note: protagonist learning the ropes and rising to power in a politically hostile environment, against their own value system — the Baru parallel. ▸① Mind

How the mind works

The machinery under everything else on this list — how you actually think, decide, and feel, versus how you assume you do. The rigorous end; all thesis, little filler.

Thinking, Fast and Slow

Real thesis

Daniel Kahneman

The foundational text on the two-system mind and the biases baked into it — the book every lesser "decision-making" title cribs from. Dense and long, but it's the substantive source, and it rewards the slow read you give the annotate tier. If you liked Think Again's questioning-your-own-reasoning move, this is the deep version.

How Emotions Are Made

Real thesis

Lisa Feldman Barrett

A genuine paradigm-shifter: emotions aren't hardwired reactions you detect, they're constructed by your brain from prediction and context. Overturns the intuitive model completely — squarely in your Ways of Being / Think Again reframe-my-assumptions wheelhouse. Rigorous, occasionally dense, worth the effort.

Bookshop Libby · Berkeley Libby · Oakland

The Righteous Mind

Real thesis

Jonathan Haidt

Why people divide over morality and politics — the "elephant and rider" model of intuition-first, reasoning-after. Connects your interest in bias (Race After Technology, Think Again) to moral psychology. Even-handed and genuinely mind-changing about how your own convictions form.

Real thesis

The findings from the Harvard Study of Adult Development — the longest longitudinal study of happiness ever run — and its blunt conclusion: relationships, more than achievement or wealth, determine how well a life goes.
